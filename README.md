1.1 Executive Summary & Core Mission
The Hybrid Backsliding Index (HBI) is an empirical, open-science auditing platform developed by the Citizens Liberty Alliance to measure institutional degradation, executive overreach, and rule-of-law erosion in constitutional republics. Public discourse surrounding democratic decline is frequently compromised by two systemic weaknesses:

1. Partisan Subjectivity: Reliance on political commentary and rhetoric rather than verifiable legal and financial records.

2. The "Salami-Slicing" Blind Spot: Institutional erosion rarely occurs through sudden overthrows. Instead, it proceeds through incremental, legalistic administrative shifts—dozens of sub-threshold budget diversions, civil service reclassifications, and regulatory adjustments that quietly bypass single-event reporting thresholds.
The HBI platform eliminates subjective bias by anchoring every metric in Level 3 Primary Data extracted directly from live federal ledgers: the Federal Register, Treasury Account Symbol (TAS) balances on USAspending.gov, Census TIGER/Line GIS shapefiles, and PACER court dockets.

1.3 Operational Security (OpSec) & Digital Footprint Protection Protocol
Independent researchers auditing public power must protect their digital footprint to maintain objectivity and prevent retaliation. A single careless mistake can link your public research profile to your private life. Analysts operating under this framework must adhere to these strict, practical standards:

 OPSEC & IDENTITY PROTECTION ARCHITECTURE

│     IDENTITY SEGREGATION     │     DATA TRACKING DEFENSES   │      ACCOUNT HARDENING       │
├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ • Zero personal links        │ • Strip tracking pixels      │ • Hardware Security Key/2FA  │
│ • Distinct institutional IDs │ • Block third-party cookies  │ • Burner forwarder aliases   │

└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘

1. Identity Segregation (No Personal Links)
Never cross-contaminate your data. Do not use your research email, username, or phone number as a recovery method for your personal bank, private social media, Amazon, or Spotify accounts. Data brokers scrape this information, and a single linked phone number can expose your real identity across the internet. Keep your "Investigator" identity in a completely separate digital bucket from your "Personal" identity.

2. Distinct IDs & Neutral Bios
Do not include Personal Identifying Information (PII) in your public GitHub, social media, or project bios. Avoid mentioning your hometown, your alma mater, your employer, or your personal political grievances. A bio should not be a diary; it is a shield. Use strict, professional descriptors only (e.g., "Independent Institutional Researcher" or "Quantitative Data Analyst").

3. Burner Forwarder Aliases
Never give out your actual root email address when registering for public forums, newsletters, API keys, or data portals. Use email forwarding services (like Apple's "Hide My Email", DuckDuckGo Email Protection, or SimpleLogin). These services generate unique, disposable email addresses (e.g., ⁠randomword77@icloud.com⁠) that forward messages to your main inbox invisibly. If a database is breached or someone tries to target that address, you simply delete the burner alias and your real email remains hidden.

4. Tracking Defenses (Pixels & IP Addresses)

In your email settings, you must enable "Ask before displaying external images." Senders routinely embed invisible 1x1 tracking pixels in emails to log your exact IP address and physical location the moment you open the message. Furthermore, use privacy-focused browsers (like Brave or Firefox with strict tracking protection) to conduct your research, preventing third-party cookies from following you across government websites.

5. Account Hardening (No SMS 2FA)
Enforce an Authenticator App for Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) (using apps like Aegis, Google Authenticator, or Bitwarden) or use a physical Hardware Token (like a YubiKey). Never rely on SMS text-message 2FA. SMS is highly vulnerable to "SIM-swap" attacks, where bad actors convince your phone carrier to port your number to their phone, immediately granting them access to your text messages and accounts.

2.1 Course Syllabus & 15-Week Lecture Schedule
Course Details: POLI 485 / LAW 720: Democratic Backsliding & Institutional Auditing
Instructional Format: 3 Hours Lecture + 2 Hours Supervised Data Lab per week.
Pedagogical Objectives:

1. Theoretical Mastery: Analyze mechanisms of democratic backsliding, including constitutional hardball and preference falsification.

2. Empirical Precision: Extract, clean, and verify primary administrative data using Python and keyless federal APIs.

3. Mathematical Application: Calculate quantitative indicators (e.g., Unilateral Budget Diversions, Efficiency Gaps, Coercion Ratios).

4. Adversarial Verification: Perform dual-coder reliability testing to maintain a data agreement score >95%.

5. Actionable Policy Translation: Translate verified datasets into formal oversight memoranda for submission to administrative bodies.
15-Week Lecture & Lab Schedule:
 
 Week 1-2 (Foundations & Scoping): Competitive authoritarianism; the 4-Tier Due Diligence Spectrum; the Rule of Stop.
 
 Week 3-5 (Module 1 - Executive Power): Article II vs. Article I; the National Emergencies Act; calculating Unilateral Budget Diversions (\bm{R_{bd}}); Salami-Slicing Cumulative Velocity (\bm{V_{cs}}).

 Week 6-7 (Module 2 - Law Enforcement): The Merit Systems Protection framework; Schedule F exceptions; selective regulatory audits; civil service attrition index (\bm{P_{cs}}).
 
 Week 8-9 (Module 3 - Franchise Degradation): The Elections Clause; NVRA 90-Day Quiet Period purges; calculating the Partisan Efficiency Gap (\bm{EG}).
 
 Week 10-11 (Module 4 - Public Sphere): Government Speech vs. Coercion Doctrine; executive "jawboning"; measuring federal advertising distortion (\bm{S_{ad}}).

 Week 12-13 (Module 5 - Judicial Decay): Article III power; single-judge division venue manipulation; quantifying executive injunction defiance (\bm{J_{ci}}).
 
 Week 14-15 (Synthesis & Capstone): Aggregating sub-indices into the 100-point composite score; delivering verified 4-page oversight memoranda to oversight bodies.
